Overview

  • 12 samples in 3 batches; every batch holds every condition (6 unstim and 6 stim), so condition and batch are not confounded.
  • 334,000 events in all; per sample median 30,000 (range 4,000–30,000).
  • 7 markers kept (5 used for clustering, 2 state markers; arcsinh cofactor 6000), plus 1 scatter/time/other channels.
  • 1 channel(s) dropped because the panel differs between samples: CH9-A (CD28).
  • Donors (from the 'donor' column of samples.csv): unstim: donor D1 in every batch; stim: donor D2 (b1, b2) and donor D3 (b3).
Keep in mind
  • b1_unstim1 (4,000 events) has fewer than a fifth of the median events per sample; proportions of rare populations in it rest on few cells.
  • No donor appears in more than one condition, so every difference between conditions is also a difference between donors; the data cannot separate the two.
  • Samples within a condition are repeated measurements of few donors (6 unstim samples from 1 donor and 6 stim samples from 2 donors). Tests that treat each sample as an independent replicate measure reproducibility across batches and aliquots, not variation between people.
  • In stim samples the donor changes with the batch, so a batch difference there is partly a donor difference.

Differential abundance

diffcyt edgeR, batch as a blocking factor; significant at FDR < 0.05.

  • stim vs unstim: 3 of 5 tested metaclusters differ in abundance at FDR < 0.05 (2 more abundant in stim, 1 less).
  • MC3 NK rose from 10% to 24% of cells (log2FC +1.26, FDR 5e-12; in all 3 batches).
  • MC2 CD8 T rose from 25% to 30% of cells (log2FC +0.28, FDR 0.006; in 1 of 3 batches).
  • MC1 CD4 T fell from 40% to 26% of cells (log2FC -0.64, FDR 0.03; in all 3 batches).
Keep in mind
  • Not every batch goes the same way for MC2 CD8 T (1 of 3); the grey lines in the figure show which.
  • One batch carries most of the difference for MC2 CD8 T (b2, 80%); these results rest largely on that batch's samples.
  • The conditions come from different donors (unstim: donor D1 in every batch; stim: donor D2 (b1, b2) and donor D3 (b3)), so these differences mix the effect of the condition with differences between donors, and the samples in a condition are aliquots and batches of the same donor(s) rather than independent people. The FDRs are therefore much smaller than a study with one sample per donor would give.
  • Proportions are compositional: MC3 NK grew by 14 percentage points, which by itself lowers every other population's share. A fall in proportion is not necessarily a fall in absolute numbers.
  • The log2 fold change is the edgeR model coefficient (log2 fold change), adjusted for batch as a fixed effect; the percentages beside it are plain means of the per-sample proportions, so the two need not match exactly.

Differential state

diffcyt limma on per-sample median expression of the state markers within each metacluster; significant at FDR < 0.05.

  • stim vs unstim: 5 of 10 tested cluster × marker pairs differ at FDR < 0.05 (5 higher in stim, 0 lower).
  • Largest shift: CD25 in MC4 CD4 T CD25+FOXP3+ (Treg or activated), 0.50 → 1.70 (median arcsinh; difference +1.20, FDR 2e-6).
  • Markers with the most significant clusters: CD25 (3) and GZMB (2).
Keep in mind
  • As for abundance, the conditions come from different donors; a shift in expression mixes the condition with the donor.
  • diffcyt's logFC for state markers is a difference in median arcsinh-transformed expression (level minus reference), not a log fold change. At high intensity arcsinh behaves like a natural log, so a difference of 1 there is roughly a 2.7-fold change in signal; near zero it is much less.

Batch effects

Earth mover's distance (EMD) between each batch's marker distributions and the pooled others, from batch-assess; 0 = identical distributions. One assessment per data version: raw, cytonorm, cycombine.

  • Before correction (raw): median EMD 0.150 (reference comparison); flagged batches: b1.
  • The most distinct batch before correction is b1 (score 0.390; 7 flagged markers, including CD3, CD4, CD8, CD56, CD25 and FOXP3).
  • After cytonorm: median EMD 0.045, 70% lower than raw; batch-vs-batch median 0.150 → 0.045 (70% lower).
  • b1 remains the most distinct batch after cytonorm (score 0.117, other batches 0.039–0.039).
  • After cycombine: median EMD 0.075, 50% lower than raw; batch-vs-batch median 0.150 → 0.075 (50% lower).
  • b1 remains the most distinct batch after cycombine (score 0.195, other batches 0.065–0.065).
  • Batch vs batch within stim samples (donor changes with batch): median EMD 0.150 (raw) → 0.045 (cytonorm), 0.075 (cycombine); lowest after cytonorm.
  • Batch vs batch within unstim samples (one donor in every batch): median EMD 0.150 (raw) → 0.045 (cytonorm), 0.075 (cycombine); lowest after cytonorm.
  • On these numbers cytonorm brought the batches closest together (70% lower median EMD than raw, and also in the batch-vs-batch comparison).
  • Markers flagged in at least one batch after every correction: CD25, CD3, CD4, CD56, CD8, FOXP3 and GZMB.
  • The unstim samples are the same donor in every batch, so differences between batches there are technical; the reference samples (unstim1) are among them, which makes the reference comparison a clean measure of the batch effect.
Keep in mind
  • Flags are relative to each run's own spread (median + k·MAD across batches, with a floor), so a batch that is still flagged after correction stands out among the corrected batches; it need not have a large shift. Compare the EMD values, which are on one scale in the figure below.
  • CytoNorm learns its correction from the reference (control) samples of each batch (unstim1); the reference comparison measures those same samples, so it shows how well the model fits its training data. The batch-vs-batch comparison (all samples within a condition) and CytoNorm's held-out replicates (below, when present) are the fairer checks.
  • cyCombine's output is a subsample (up to 50,000 cells per sample), so its assessment covers fewer cells than the others.
  • cyCombine was given the 'condition' column as a covariate, so differences between conditions were protected from the correction.
  • In the batch-vs-batch comparison the stim samples change donor with the batch (D2 in b1, b2 and D3 in b3), so part of their EMD is a donor difference that no batch correction should remove.
